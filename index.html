Why "no food after 8pm" isn't the real lever

The idea: stop eating by a certain hour and you'll lose weight.
What actually happens: the body responds to total energy in vs. total energy out — it doesn't track the clock.

What actually drives weight change

Weight loss happens when you consistently take in fewer calories than you burn, averaged over days and weeks — not measured against any single meal or cutoff time. A 7pm dinner and a 9pm dinner with identical calories have the same effect on weight.

Why the "8pm rule" seems to work anyway

It's usually indirect. A cutoff time removes the window where a lot of extra eating happens — snacks in front of a screen, second helpings out of boredom. Cut that window and intake often drops without anyone tracking anything. The clock isn't the mechanism; it's just a simple rule that happens to reduce intake for some people.

Why "weekly," not "daily," is the useful lens

Day-to-day intake naturally swings — a birthday dinner, a stressful week, a big workout. Judging progress by a single day invites frustration for no reason. Looking at the week smooths that noise out and shows the actual trend, which is what the Week tab here is built around.

A rough starting point

A sustained deficit of about 500 kcal/day tends to track with roughly 0.5kg of loss per week for most people — a rough model, not a guarantee, since metabolism, water, and hormones all move the number around. Consistency over weeks matters far more than any single day's total.
